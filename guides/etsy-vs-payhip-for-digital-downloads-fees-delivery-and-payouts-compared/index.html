Etsy vs Payhip for Digital Downloads: Fees, Delivery and Payouts Compared

Originally published on Telegraph

Outline

  • Fees side by side
  • Listing and digital delivery
  • Payouts and cash timing
  • When each platform fits a printable or planner seller
  • Conclusion

If you sell printables or planners, two common paths are listing on Etsy or selling from your own storefront on Payhip. Both handle digital delivery. They differ on how buyers find you, what you pay per sale, how you upload files, and when money reaches your bank.

This comparison sticks to each company's published help and pricing pages. Fees change, so check the linked sources before you rely on any number. Nothing here is financial, legal or tax advice, and nothing promises income.

I sell digital planners on Payhip, including the Calm Money Monthly Planner. The goal of this post is a clear side-by-side, not a sales pitch for either option.

Fees side by side

Etsy. Etsy's help center lists a $0.20 listing fee per listing. That fee is charged when you list or renew, whether or not the item sells, and listings expire after four months. On each sale, Etsy charges a 6.5% transaction fee on the order amount (including shipping and gift wrap if you charge them; for US sellers, sales tax is treated separately on that page). On top of that, Etsy Payments processing for a United States bank account is 3% + $0.25 per order, taken from the total sale price including shipping and any sales tax. Optional ads (Offsite Ads, Etsy Ads) can add more cost if you use them.

Payhip. Payhip's pricing page has three plans with the same features: Free Forever at $0/month plus a 5% transaction fee; Plus at $29/month plus 2%; Pro at $99/month with no Payhip transaction fee. Payhip states that PayPal and Stripe still charge at their standard rates on every plan. Stripe's published US rate for domestic cards is 2.9% + 30¢ per successful transaction; PayPal publishes its own tables on its business fees page.

Illustrative $10 digital sale (US card, no ads, ignoring listing amortization). On Etsy: 6.5% ($0.65) + 3% + $0.25 processing ($0.55) ≈ $1.20 in sale-based fees, plus the separate $0.20 when you list or renew. On Payhip Free with Stripe at 2.9% + 30¢: 5% ($0.50) + about $0.59 ≈ $1.09. Plus lowers the platform cut to 2% but adds $29/month. Real take-home still depends on volume, ads, refunds and which processor you use.

In short: Etsy stacks listing + marketplace transaction + processing. Payhip uses a plan-based percentage (or a monthly fee that lowers or removes it) plus PayPal or Stripe.

Listing and digital delivery

Etsy. When you create a listing, you choose Digital files. Instant downloads require you to upload the files before publishing: up to five files, each up to 20 MB, in supported types such as PDF, JPG, PNG and ZIP. Buyers get the files right after purchase. Made-to-order digital listings skip the upfront upload; you complete the order later by uploading up to five files of 20 MB each. Digital items must be made and/or designed by you. Etsy also notes you can't offer variations on digital items.

Payhip. You add a digital product, upload files (Payhip's help says each file can be up to 5 GB), set a price and cover, then publish. After checkout, Payhip sends the buyer to a download page and emails a receipt with the download link for later access. Payment goes to your connected PayPal or Stripe account, and Payhip emails you about the sale.

For a typical printable PDF under 20 MB, both deliver instantly. The practical gaps are file size (Payhip allows much larger files), Etsy's five-file cap per listing, and discovery: Etsy is marketplace search; Payhip is your storefront, so buyers usually arrive from your own links, blog or social posts.

Payouts and cash timing

Etsy. Sales land in your Etsy Payment account first. You choose a deposit schedule: daily, weekly, biweekly or monthly. New sellers generally see funds eligible for deposit about 14 days after a sale; established shops may see eligibility as soon as the next business day, depending on shop standing. After Etsy initiates a deposit, banks often take 1–5 business days for a standard transfer (instant transfers are described separately on that page).

Payhip. Payhip says it deposits sales immediately after a transaction is completed, into your PayPal or Stripe account. From there, payout timing follows that processor's own schedule—not Payhip's calendar.

If you need predictable weekly deposits into a bank account inside a marketplace wallet, Etsy's schedule is the clearer story. If you want funds to hit PayPal or Stripe as soon as the sale clears, Payhip's model is simpler.

When each platform fits a printable or planner seller

Etsy tends to fit when:

  • You want buyers who are already browsing that marketplace.
  • You're fine paying listing fees and the combined transaction + processing cut.
  • Your files fit the five-file / 20 MB-per-file rules for instant downloads.
  • You're okay waiting for funds to clear into Etsy's payment account on their schedule.

Payhip tends to fit when:

  • You already drive traffic (blog, newsletter, social, Pinterest) to your own product links.
  • You want one storefront with plan-based fees and no per-listing charge.
  • You need larger file sizes or a simpler "your brand, your URL" checkout.
  • You prefer money landing in PayPal or Stripe right after the sale.

Some sellers use both: Etsy for discovery, Payhip for owned traffic. Whether that is worth the extra tracking depends on how you market. A simple monthly sheet like the Calm Money Monthly Planner can keep gross, fees and net in one place.

Conclusion

Etsy is a marketplace: $0.20 listing, 6.5% transaction, country-based processing (3% + $0.25 in the US), and scheduled deposits. Payhip is a direct storefront: Free (5%), Plus ($29 + 2%) or Pro ($99, 0% Payhip fee), plus PayPal or Stripe, with funds deposited to that processor after a completed sale. Digital delivery works on both; file limits and how buyers find you matter more day to day.

Check the fee and help pages below, run the math on a price you actually charge, and pick the setup that matches how you get traffic.
