Intro: a popular digital product with fine print

Editable templates for things like social posts, invitations, planners and media kits are a popular digital product, and Canva is a common place to build them. But every element in Canva's library comes with a license, and that license decides how you're allowed to deliver a template to a buyer.

This post walks through what Canva's own Content License Agreement and Help Center say. It's a plain-language summary, not legal advice. Canva says it can change these terms, so check the current versions before you launch [Canva, Content License Agreement].

Free Content vs Pro Content: the distinction that matters most

Canva's library includes photos, icons, illustrations, video, audio, fonts and templates, which the agreement calls "Content" [Canva, Content License Agreement]. The main categories, from most to least restrictive, are Education Content, Branded Content, Pro Content and Free Content. If a design mixes categories, the most restrictive one applies to the whole design [Canva, Content License Agreement].

That last point trips people up. A template that is 95% Free Content and one Pro element is treated as a Pro design.

You can check where an element comes from by hovering over it in the Elements panel and clicking the three dots to see its source information [Canva, Content License Agreement].

Rule 1: Templates must be your original design

Canva's Help Center says templates you sell need to be an original design, and that you can use a mix of Free and Pro Content [Canva Help, Products for sale]. In its example of a self-published children's book, Canva says starting from a Canva template is fine as long as the creator isn't selling the template as-is and adds their own content and creative effort so the design is unique [Canva Help, Products for sale].

The practical takeaway: don't relist or lightly recolor a Canva template. Build the layout yourself.

Rule 2: Pro Content means template links only

This is the rule most sellers need to know. According to Canva's Help Center:

  • If your template includes Pro Content, you can only sell it as a Canva template link, which brings your customer into Canva to edit their own copy.
  • You can't sell templates with Pro Content as a PDF or other file type, whether flattened or not [Canva Help, Products for sale].

The license agreement backs this up. It lists "design templates solely for use on Canva" as a permitted use [Canva, Content License Agreement], and it prohibits redistributing Content as a standalone item or in a product that lets someone extract or reproduce it as an electronic file [Canva, Content License Agreement].

Rule 3: Free-only templates can be sold as files

Free Content has fewer restrictions. The agreement lets you use Free Content in templates for distribution or sale to third parties, including design templates intended for resale, such as business card or brochure templates [Canva, Content License Agreement]. It also says that if a design contains any Pro Content, these extra Free Content permissions don't apply [Canva, Content License Agreement].

Canva's Help Center gives an example of a seller who makes wedding invitation templates for Etsy. Templates with Pro Content are allowed only as template links. Templates using only Free Content and the seller's own photos can be sold as PDFs or as template links [Canva Help, Products for sale].

One caveat: Free Content labeled CC0 or Public Domain is covered by its own license, and Free Content labeled Branded or Education Content follows those stricter rules [Canva, Content License Agreement]. Check the source information, not just the "free" label.

How to create a template link

Canva says creating and sharing a template link is available on Canva Pro, Teams, Enterprise, Canva for Education and Canva for Nonprofits [Canva Help, Template link]. The steps it lists:

  • Open the design.
  • Select Share in the menu bar above the editor.
  • Select Template link (choose "See all" first if you don't see it).
  • Select Create template link, then Copy.

Anyone with the link can view and use the design as a template [Canva Help, Template link]. Canva notes one exception: if you create the link from a work email domain, recipients with that same domain need a paid plan to open it [Canva Help, Template link]. If you delete a template link, that URL stops working, and making a new link creates a new URL [Canva Help, Template link]. Keep that in mind before deleting a link that buyers have already received.

Many sellers deliver the link inside a simple PDF or a download page on their storefront. Test the link in a private browser window before you publish your listing.

Note that education accounts are a special case. The agreement says Canva Education users may only use Pro Content for educational, non-commercial purposes, and should create a separate account for commercial designs [Canva, Content License Agreement].

Content you shouldn't use in products for sale

Some Content categories aren't for commercial use at all:

  • Branded Content is personal-use only unless its source information says otherwise, and you can't use it to create merchandise for sale [Canva, Content License Agreement].
  • Education Content, marked with a "Resource" badge, is for educational, non-commercial use only [Canva, Content License Agreement].
  • Disney templates and elements are for personal or educational use only and can't be used commercially [Canva, Content License Agreement].
  • "Editorial Use Only" content can't be used for commercial or promotional purposes [Canva, Content License Agreement].

The agreement also says you can't use any Content as part of a trademark, business name or logo (fonts excepted) [Canva, Content License Agreement]. So a "logo template" product built from Canva graphics is risky for your buyers.

Other limits worth knowing

  • One design per Pro license. Each Pro Content license covers use in one Canva design. Subscribers get a new license automatically each time they export a new design with that Content [Canva, Content License Agreement].
  • Pixel limit for e-books and web use. Unedited Pro Content in online or electronic publications such as e-books is capped at 480,000 total pixels per Content file (for example, 600 x 800 px), with some exceptions for Canva-published websites and embeds [Canva, Content License Agreement].
  • No AI training. The agreement prohibits using Content for machine learning or AI purposes [Canva, Content License Agreement].
  • Client work is different. You can transfer a design to one client for their own use under a written agreement, but that's not the same as selling a template to many buyers [Canva, Content License Agreement].

A pre-launch checklist

  • Is the layout my original design, not a lightly edited Canva template?
  • Have I checked the source of every element (Free, Pro, Branded, Education, CC0)?
  • If there's any Pro Content, am I delivering only a template link, not a PDF or PNG?
  • Have I avoided Branded, Education, Disney and Editorial Use Only content?
  • Is my template link created from the right account and tested in a private window?
  • Does my listing say buyers will edit the template in Canva, so they know what they're getting?

Conclusion

Selling Canva templates can be a reasonable digital product, but delivery format is not a detail. If your design uses any Pro Content, sell it as a template link. If you want to sell downloadable files, build with Free Content and your own uploads only, and confirm each element's source. Read the current license before you launch, because Canva can update it [Canva, Content License Agreement].
