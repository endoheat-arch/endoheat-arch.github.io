Payhip Discounts Done Right: Coupons, Sale Prices, Cross-Sells and Referral Offers for Printable Sellers

If you sell printables or other digital downloads on Payhip, sooner or later you'll want to run a promotion: a launch week, a holiday sale, a thank-you for repeat buyers. Payhip gives you several different discount tools, and they behave differently. Picking the right one keeps your offer clear to buyers and keeps you from accidentally giving away more than you meant to.

Everything below comes from Payhip's own Help Center and from the U.S. Federal Trade Commission's pricing guides, linked inline and in the Sources section. Platform features change, so check those pages when you set yours up.

General information only. This is not financial, legal or tax advice, and no discount strategy guarantees sales.

Why discounts deserve a plan, not a panic button

A discount works best when it has a reason and an end. "Launch week," "back-to-school," or "for newsletter subscribers" tells a buyer why the price is lower today. Payhip itself points out that an expiry date is "great for adding some urgency to your promotion" (Coupons). Before touching any settings, write down three things: who the offer is for, what it applies to, and when it stops.

Option 1: a simple "on sale" price

The most basic tool lives on the product itself. Payhip's beginner guide explains that if you want to run a sales promotion, you can check the box marked "Product is on sale" and set a price below the usual value (How to Sell Your First Product on Payhip).

This suits a store-wide or single-product sale that everyone should see without needing a code. The trade-off is that it is public. You can't limit it to one group of buyers, and you have to remember to switch it off.

Option 2: coupon codes, and the settings that matter

Coupons are the most flexible option. You create them under Marketing, which opens on the Coupons page by default (Coupons). The settings worth knowing:

  • What it applies to. A coupon can cover the total order amount, one specific product, or one collection (Coupons).
  • Your own code. Payhip generates a code automatically, but you can replace it with something meaningful. Its example is a code like "1YEAR" given only to customers who've been with you for over a year (Coupons).
  • Percentage or fixed amount. Choose a percentage off the original price or a set amount deducted (Coupons).
  • Usage limit. This caps how many times the code can be used overall, not per customer. Payhip suggests separate single-use codes if you want each person to use one only once (Coupons).
  • Expiry date in UTC. A coupon set to expire on January 1st stops working at 00:00 UTC that day (Coupons). If you're on U.S. Pacific time, that's the afternoon or evening before, so set the date one day later than you think if you promised "through midnight."
  • Duration for recurring plans. When a coupon applies to a recurring or payment-plan price, you can choose Forever, Once, or a set number of months (Coupons).
  • Private notes and bulk codes. You can add internal notes customers never see, and bulk create up to 50 unique codes per session (Coupons).

If you work with code, Payhip's API also exposes the same ideas, including a start date, end date, minimum purchase amount and usage limit (Payhip API: Create a coupon). The minimum purchase option is handy for "spend $10, save $3" offers.

A 100% coupon has two practical uses. Payhip recommends it for giving someone free access to a paid product, such as a reviewer copy or a customer moving over from another platform (Give Access to a Product). It's also the easiest way to run a test purchase, covered below.

Option 3: cross-sells that discount the second item

A cross-sell shows a discounted add-on when someone puts a product in their cart. Payhip's example: a buyer adds one book, and a prompt offers a second book at 25% off (Cross Selling). You set it up under Marketing, then Cross Selling, by choosing a "trigger product" and the "promoted products" that appear with it (Cross Selling).

For printable sellers, this pairs naturally: a monthly planner as the trigger, matching trackers as the promoted add-on. A few details from Payhip's guide are easy to miss:

  • Cross-sells offer the discount before checkout, while Payhip's upgrade discounts make an offer after checkout is complete (Cross Selling).
  • If you set up cross-sells both ways between two products, Payhip by default applies only the higher discount. Turning on "circular reference" adds the two percentages together instead (Cross Selling).
  • The optional "prominent popup" uses the product's first cover image, but Payhip notes some customers may not like popups and suggests watching conversions before and after (Cross Selling).
  • On mobile, a buyer who clicks Buy Now sees no cross-sell at all (Cross Selling).

Courses, memberships, bundles and coaching products use a separate cross-sell flow that offers add-ons after the main purchase (Cross Selling For Courses, Memberships And More).

Option 4: referral discounts that buyers share for you

Payhip's referral system lets a buyer share a link that gives a friend a discount, and rewards the original buyer too. In Payhip's 25% example, each friend gets a unique single-use code that expires in 24 hours, and the referrer's discount grows with each friend who buys, up to a 100% off code after four purchases (Referral System). The referrer has one week to use their reward, and that window refreshes each time another friend buys (Referral System).

This fits low-priced printables people genuinely recommend to each other. Do the math on your chosen percentage before turning it on, because a generous rate stacks up fast.

Pay What You Want as a different kind of offer

Pay What You Want isn't a discount, but it's another way to lower the barrier. Type a "+" after the price: "0+" lets buyers pay any amount including nothing, and "5+" sets a $5 minimum (Pay What You Want Pricing). It's available only for digital and physical products. Payhip also notes that Stripe doesn't allow its platform to be used for donations or tips without offering something in return (Pay What You Want Pricing).

Keep your "was" prices honest

If you advertise "Was $12, now $8," the FTC's Guides Against Deceptive Pricing are worth a read. They say a former-price comparison is legitimate when the former price is the actual, bona fide price the item was offered at on a regular basis for a reasonably substantial period of time. Raising a price briefly just to "cut" it creates a false bargain (16 CFR 233.1). The same guide warns against tiny "reductions" that imply a bigger saving than you're offering, such as "Reduced to $9.99" from $10 (16 CFR Part 233).

The simple rule: only show a "regular price" you actually charged.

Test the whole thing before you announce it

Payhip's suggestion for testing checkout is to create a 100% off coupon, click Buy Now on your product, apply the code and complete the purchase. Afterward, delete the coupon or limit its usage (Testing Checkout Flow). Keep in mind that test orders appear on your Customers page and can't be deleted (Add Digital Products).

A short checklist for your next promotion

  • Decide who the offer is for and why it exists right now
  • Pick one tool: sale price for everyone, coupon for a group, cross-sell for a natural pair
  • Set a usage limit and an expiry date, remembering the UTC cut-off
  • Use private notes so you remember what each code was for
  • Check any "was" price is one you really charged
  • Run a test purchase, then disable the test coupon

If you sell something like the Calm Money Monthly Planner, a natural first promotion is a cross-sell: the planner as the trigger product, with a matching worksheet offered at a modest discount. Buyers who already want one can easily take home the set.
