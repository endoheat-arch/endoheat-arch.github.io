Intro: the fee is part of your price

Freelance marketplaces bring you clients, payment handling and dispute support, and they charge for it. The catch is that each platform charges differently: one takes a flat cut, one sets a fee per contract, and one has a minimum that hits small jobs hardest. If you don't know the rules, a "$100 job" can mean very different take-home amounts.

This post compares the freelancer-side fees of Upwork, Fiverr and Freelancer.com, using each company's own help pages. Fees change, so check the current page before you rely on any number here. Nothing below is advice on which platform you should use or a promise of what you'll earn.

Upwork: a per-contract service fee plus Connects

Upwork charges a Freelancer Service Fee on your earnings. According to Upwork's help center, the fee ranges from 0% to 15% per contract [Upwork, Service Fee]. You see the percentage before you commit, when you submit a proposal or receive an offer, and once a contract starts the fee is locked and won't change [Upwork, Service Fee].

Some details worth knowing:

  • Refunds: if money is refunded to a client, Upwork returns the service fee you paid on that amount [Upwork, Service Fee].
  • No regular discounts: Upwork says it doesn't offer regular fee discounts, though it may run occasional promotions [Upwork, Service Fee].
  • Exceptions: Upwork Payroll and Any Hire contracts carry no freelancer service fee, Direct Contracts with clients you bring yourself have a reduced fee, and Enterprise clients typically mean a 10% fee [Upwork, Service Fee].

Upwork's help page includes its own worked example: to take home $20 an hour on a contract with a 10% fee, you would bill about $22.22 an hour [Upwork, Service Fee].

Connects. Submitting proposals on Upwork uses Connects, a virtual token. Upwork says Connects cost $0.15 each and are sold in bundles [Upwork, Connects]. A free Basic account may include 10 Connects a month, while Freelancer Plus costs $19.99 a month and includes 100 [Upwork, Is Upwork Free]. Upwork's comparison also lists Direct Contracts at a 5% service fee on Basic and 0% on Freelancer Plus [Upwork, Is Upwork Free]. Bidding has a real cost even when you don't win the job.

Fiverr: a flat 20% and a clearing period

Fiverr is simpler on the seller side. Its help center says that for every completed order, you earn 80% of the purchase amount, including Gig Extras and tips [Fiverr, Earnings page]. In other words, Fiverr keeps 20%, and there's no per-contract variation.

Timing matters too. Fiverr says revenue becomes available 14 days after an order is completed, or 7 days for Seller Plus, Top Rated and Pro freelancers [Fiverr, Managing orders]. Eligible freelancers can skip the clearing period for a 1% fee [Fiverr, Earnings page].

Fiverr also charges buyers a separate service fee at checkout, 5.5% on orders according to its client help page [Fiverr, How Fiverr works for clients]. That doesn't come out of your earnings, but it does affect what clients pay in total.

Freelancer.com: 10% with a $5 minimum

Freelancer.com's fees page states that the freelancer fee for fixed-price projects is 10% or $5.00 USD, whichever is greater, and 10% of each payment for hourly projects [Freelancer.com, Fees]. Contest prizes carry the same 10% or $5 fee, charged only when you win and hand over the work [Freelancer.com, Fees].

A few other items from the same page:

  • Preferred Freelancer Program: Recruiter projects carry a 15% fee [Freelancer.com, Fees].
  • Optional upgrades: paid bid and project upgrades are optional [Freelancer.com, Fees].
  • Withdrawals and inactivity: withdrawal fees depend on the method, and an account that hasn't logged in for six months can be charged a monthly maintenance fee, refunded if you reactivate [Freelancer.com, Fees].

The $5 minimum is the thing to watch on small jobs. On a $20 fixed-price project, 10% would be $2, so the $5 minimum applies, which works out to 25% of the job.

Side-by-side on the same job

Here's the platform fee alone, using each company's published rules. It ignores withdrawal costs, currency conversion and taxes, and for Upwork it assumes a 10% fee, which falls within its 0% to 15% range. Your actual Upwork fee is whatever your contract shows.

  • $20 job: Upwork at 10%, $2 fee. Fiverr 20%, $4 fee. Freelancer.com fixed-price, $5 fee (the minimum).
  • $100 job: Upwork at 10%, $10. Fiverr, $20. Freelancer.com, $10.
  • $1,000 job: Upwork at 10%, $100. Fiverr, $200. Freelancer.com, $100.

The pattern: Fiverr's flat 20% is the largest cut at every size, Freelancer.com's minimum stings on very small jobs, and Upwork depends on the specific contract.

The costs that aren't in the headline percentage

Comparing percentages alone can mislead. Other costs show up too:

  • Getting work: Upwork proposals cost Connects [Upwork, Connects], and Freelancer.com projects come through bidding, while Fiverr buyers find and order your gig. Your time spent pitching is a cost even when it's not on an invoice.
  • Cash-flow timing: Fiverr's 14-day clearing period [Fiverr, Managing orders] means waiting for money you've already earned.
  • Memberships: Upwork's Freelancer Plus is $19.99 a month [Upwork, Is Upwork Free]. It may or may not pay for itself depending on how much you bid.
  • Withdrawal fees: each platform has its own payout options and charges [Freelancer.com, Fees].
  • Taxes: platform fees don't cover what you may owe. In the U.S., the IRS sets the self-employment tax rate at 15.3% [IRS].

A simple way to price with fees in mind

Upwork's own example shows the basic math: divide the amount you want to keep by (1 minus the fee rate) [Upwork, Service Fee]. With a 10% fee, keeping $20 an hour means billing about $22.22. With Fiverr's 20%, keeping $40 on a gig means listing it at $50. With Freelancer.com, check whether the $5 minimum applies before quoting a small fixed-price job.

A few habits help:

  • Write down the fee on every contract, especially on Upwork where it varies.
  • Track the hours you spend pitching, not just delivering.
  • Recheck fee pages every few months, since platforms update them.

Conclusion

No platform is "free." Upwork charges a per-contract fee of 0% to 15% plus the cost of Connects. Fiverr takes a flat 20% and holds funds for a clearing period. Freelancer.com takes 10% with a $5 minimum on fixed-price work. The best fit depends on how you find clients, the size of your typical job, and how quickly you need to be paid. Read each platform's current fee page, do the math on a real job you'd take, and decide from there.
