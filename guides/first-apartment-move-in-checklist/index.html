First Apartment Move-In Checklist

Originally published on Telegraph

Getting the keys feels huge. Then the boxes hit the floor and a dozen "I should have asked" moments show up. A short move-in checklist keeps the first week calm, protects your deposit, and gets utilities and budgets under control before the chaos wins.

1. Do a walkthrough before you unpack

Walk every room with your phone camera and a notepad. Note scratches, stains, missing blinds, sticky locks, and anything that doesn't work. Date the photos. Send a polite note to the landlord or property manager with the list so pre-existing damage isn't blamed on you later. This one step saves more deposit money than almost anything else.

2. Set up utilities and internet in the first 48 hours

Call (or use the provider apps) for electricity, gas if needed, water/trash if you pay them, and internet. Write down confirmation numbers, account numbers, and the start date for each service. Keep those details in one place so you are not digging through texts when something goes wrong.

3. Change your address and key contacts

Update your bank, employer, school, insurance, subscriptions, and anyone who mails you. Forward mail for at least a few months. Add the landlord, building manager, and after-hours maintenance numbers to your phone the same day.

4. Separate one-time costs from monthly rent

Move-in money and monthly money are different piles. One-time usually includes deposit, first month, maybe last month, application fees, truck or movers, basic furniture, and cleaning supplies. Monthly is rent, utilities, internet, renter's insurance, and groceries. Write both lists once so rent week does not surprise you.

5. Buy the boring essentials first

Before decorating, get a working smoke/CO situation checked, a basic toolkit, trash bags, toilet paper, a shower curtain, cleaning spray, light bulbs, and a power strip. A short essentials list beats three expensive Target runs.

6. Give yourself a six-week runway when you can

If you still have time before move day, work backward: confirm the lease and keys, schedule the truck, measure the biggest furniture, pack a first-night box (sheets, chargers, meds, snacks), and leave buffer days for delays. Even two weeks of planning beats packing at midnight.
