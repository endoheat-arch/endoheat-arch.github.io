Intro: YouTube as a long-term income channel

For people who teach, review products or make how-to content, YouTube can become a long-term income stream. But monetization comes with specific thresholds and rules, and myths about them spread fast. This guide sums up what YouTube's own Help Center and blog say about the YouTube Partner Program (YPP) right now.

It's an overview, not a promise of earnings. YouTube itself says there are no guarantees under the partner agreement about how much, or whether, you'll be paid [YouTube Help, Partner earnings].

What the YouTube Partner Program is

YouTube says YPP gives creators more access to YouTube resources and monetization features, access to its Creator Support teams, and revenue sharing from ads served on their content [YouTube Help, YPP overview].

Ads can show on videos from channels outside YPP, too. YouTube says its Terms of Service let it serve ads on all content, but channels that don't meet YPP's criteria don't receive a share of that revenue [YouTube Help, YPP overview].

Basic requirements to join

Before thresholds even come into play, YouTube lists these requirements [YouTube Help, YPP overview]:

  • Follow YouTube's channel monetization policies.
  • Live in a country or region where YPP is available.
  • Have no active Community Guidelines strikes.
  • Turn on 2-Step Verification for your Google Account.
  • Have advanced features access on YouTube.
  • Have one active AdSense for YouTube account linked to your channel, or be ready to set one up in YouTube Studio.

The two thresholds: 500 subscribers and 1,000 subscribers

The expanded (lower) tier. In eligible countries and regions, creators can apply to YPP once they have 500 subscribers, 3 valid public uploads in the last 90 days, and either 3,000 qualified watch hours in the last 12 months or 3 million qualified Shorts views in the last 90 days [YouTube Help, Expanded YPP]. This tier unlocks fan funding and Shopping features, such as channel memberships, Super Chat, Super Stickers, Super Thanks and promoting your own products with YouTube Shopping [YouTube Blog, More ways to earn; YouTube Help, Expanded YPP].

The ad revenue tier. To share in ad revenue, a channel needs either 1,000 subscribers and 4,000 qualified watch hours in the last 12 months, or 1,000 subscribers and 10 million qualified Shorts views in the last 90 days [YouTube Help, YPP overview]. YouTube says creators who joined at the lower tier automatically become eligible for ad revenue sharing once they reach these criteria, without going through the full application again [YouTube Blog, More ways to earn].

Fan funding has its own rules too. YouTube says you need to be in YPP, be at least 18, and live where each feature is available [YouTube Help, Commerce Products policies].

What counts (and doesn't) toward watch hours and Shorts views

Only qualified watch hours from public long-form videos count toward the watch-hour threshold. Hours from private, unlisted or deleted videos, ad campaigns, Shorts, and livestreams that are unlisted, deleted or not converted to video on demand don't count [YouTube Help, YPP overview].

For Shorts, only qualified views from public Shorts in the Shorts Feed count. Views of private, unlisted or deleted Shorts, ad campaigns, and image posts in the Shorts Feed don't [YouTube Help, YPP overview]. Watch hours from Shorts views also don't count toward the 4,000-hour threshold [YouTube Help, YPP overview].

The practical takeaway: running ads to your own videos won't get you to the threshold faster, and Shorts and long-form are two separate paths.

How ad revenue is shared

Once you're in YPP, you choose how to earn by accepting specific modules in YouTube Studio [YouTube Help, YPP overview]:

  • Long-form videos (Watch Page): YouTube pays 55% of net revenues from ads shown on your public videos on the Watch Page, including when they're played in the YouTube player on other sites or apps [YouTube Help, Partner earnings].
  • Shorts: ad revenue from the Shorts Feed is pooled each month. Creators are allocated a share based on their share of eligible engaged views, and keep 45% of that allocated revenue, whether or not they used music [YouTube Help, Shorts monetization].

YouTube Premium also pays creators: when a Premium subscriber watches your content, you get part of the fee they pay [YouTube Help, Choose how to monetize].

Applying and the review process

When you're eligible, you apply in YouTube Studio under Earn: accept the base terms, then set up or link an AdSense for YouTube account [YouTube Help, YPP overview]. You can select Get notified in the Earn area to get an email when you reach a threshold [YouTube Help, YPP overview].

Meeting the threshold doesn't mean automatic acceptance. Automated systems and human reviewers look at your channel as a whole. YouTube says a decision typically takes about a month, though delays are possible [YouTube Help, YPP overview].

If you're rejected, you can appeal within 21 days, or keep uploading and reapply after 30 days. After a repeat rejection, the wait is 90 days [YouTube Help, YPP overview].

Staying monetized

Getting in isn't the end of it. YouTube says it continuously checks channels in YPP [YouTube Help, YPP overview]. It may turn off monetization on channels that haven't uploaded a video or posted to the Posts tab for 6 months or more, and channels lose monetization if they violate the monetization policies, regardless of their numbers [YouTube Help, YPP overview].

Heads-up: updated terms in early 2027

YouTube's overview page says it's introducing updates to YPP starting February 1, 2027. To keep fully monetizing, creators need to review and accept the updated terms in YouTube Studio by January 31, 2027 [YouTube Help, YPP overview]. If you're already a partner, put that date on your calendar.

A practical path for side-hustle creators

  • Pick a lane. Decide early whether you're mainly building long-form watch hours or Shorts views, since they count separately.
  • Upload consistently. The lower tier requires 3 public uploads in the last 90 days [YouTube Help, Expanded YPP].
  • Set up the basics now. Turn on 2-Step Verification and check advanced features access before you hit the numbers.
  • Plan for income beyond ads. Fan funding and Shopping open at the lower tier, and you can point viewers to your own digital products, such as templates or planners.
  • Track the threshold using the Get notified option in YouTube Studio.
  • Stay active. Avoid gaps of six months without uploads or posts once you're in.

Conclusion

The YouTube Partner Program has two doors: a 500-subscriber tier for fan funding and Shopping, and a 1,000-subscriber tier for ad revenue, with separate long-form and Shorts paths. Ad revenue shares are 55% for long-form and 45% for Shorts, but YouTube guarantees nothing about how much you'll earn. Build the channel on content you can sustain, get your account settings ready early, and check YouTube's Help Center for updates, including the new terms coming in February 2027.
