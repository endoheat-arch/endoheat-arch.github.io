Gumroad vs Payhip for Digital Downloads: Fees, Payouts and File Delivery Compared for Printable Sellers

Originally published on Telegraph

  • Payouts: when the money actually reaches you

  • File delivery and PDF protection

Gumroad and Payhip both let you sell digital downloads from your own product links without building a full website. For printable, planner and template sellers, the differences that matter most are how fees hit a low-priced product, how and when you get paid, and how files reach the buyer.

Everything below comes from each company's own pricing and help pages, checked in October 2026; confirm them before relying on any number. This is a general comparison, not financial, legal or tax advice, and nothing here promises any level of sales or income.

For transparency: I sell planners on Payhip, including the Calm Money Monthly Planner. I've tried to keep this a fair side-by-side.

Fees: per-sale vs plan-based

Gumroad. Gumroad's help center says that for sales made on its website it charges 10% + $0.50 per transaction, and that this does not include credit card processing (listed as 2.9% + $0.30) or PayPal fees. Sales that come through Gumroad's own marketplace, which it calls Discover, carry a flat 30% fee that includes processing. Gumroad's pricing page describes the 30% rate as applying when new customers find and buy from you through Discover. There is no monthly fee.

Gumroad also lists a high-volume discount: once your paid sales in a calendar month reach $20,000, new direct sales that month drop to 5% + $0.50, resetting on the 1st. Free products cost nothing in fees; Gumroad says it doesn't take any fee on free product sales.

Payhip. Payhip's pricing page lists three plans with the same features: Free Forever ($0/month + 5% transaction fee), Plus ($29/month + 2%) and Pro ($99/month, no transaction fee). Payhip states that PayPal and Stripe still charge their standard rates on every plan. Stripe's published US rate for domestic cards is 2.9% + 30¢ per successful charge.

The structural difference: Gumroad adds a fixed $0.50 to every paid sale, while Payhip's platform fee is a percentage only (or a monthly subscription that lowers or removes it). On cheap printables, that fixed amount matters.

Worked examples at $5 and $10

These are illustrations using the published rates above, assuming a US card payment, no affiliates, no discounts and ignoring sales tax. Real numbers will differ.

A $10 planner:

  • Gumroad direct sale: 10% ($1.00) + $0.50 + card processing 2.9% + $0.30 ($0.59) = about $2.09 in fees.
  • Gumroad Discover sale: 30% flat = $3.00.
  • Payhip Free with Stripe: 5% ($0.50) + Stripe ($0.59) = about $1.09.
  • Payhip Plus with Stripe: 2% ($0.20) + Stripe ($0.59) = about $0.79 per sale, plus $29 per month.

A $5 printable:

  • Gumroad direct sale: $0.50 + $0.50 + about $0.45 processing = about $1.45, or roughly 29% of the price.
  • Gumroad Discover sale: 30% = $1.50.
  • Payhip Free with Stripe: $0.25 + about $0.45 = about $0.70, or roughly 14%.

The lower your price, the more Gumroad's fixed $0.50 weighs on each sale; on higher-priced planners the gap narrows. Payhip's paid plans only pay off once the lower percentage covers the subscription, so run that math with your own numbers.

Payouts: when the money reaches you

Gumroad. On Gumroad, sales generally land in your Gumroad balance first and are paid out on a schedule. Key points from its "Getting paid" article:

  • A minimum balance of $100 USD is needed for a standard payout (some countries have higher local minimums).
  • You can choose daily, weekly, monthly or quarterly payouts. On weekly, monthly and quarterly schedules, each sale has a minimum 7-day holding period. Daily payouts are limited to eligible US accounts with at least four prior payouts.
  • US bank accounts are paid on Thursdays; PayPal payouts go out on Fridays and carry a 2% processing fee.
  • US creators who qualify can request instant payouts for a 3% fee, after at least one payout and 60 days of processing.
  • New accounts go through a review that can take 1–3 weeks.

Gumroad's Stripe Connect option, which pays card sales straight to your Stripe account, is no longer available to new users except in Brazil.

Payhip. Payhip says it deposits your sales immediately after a transaction is completed, into the PayPal or Stripe account you connect. Its help center describes the same flow: the buyer pays, and the payment is deposited to your PayPal/Stripe account. When the money then reaches your bank depends on PayPal's or Stripe's own payout schedule.

For a new seller with only a few sales a month, Gumroad's $100 minimum and holding period can mean waiting weeks for a first payout. Payhip's model puts each sale in your processor account right away.

Sales tax and VAT

Both platforms now take on much of the tax collection work, which is a real relief for small digital sellers.

  • Gumroad says it acts as the Merchant of Record for all sales and handles sales tax collection and remittance worldwide. Gumroad announced this change in a December 2024 post, effective January 1, 2025.
  • Payhip says it collects and pays digital EU/UK VAT and US/Canadian sales tax on your behalf. Its US help article says that from July 1, 2026 it handles US sales tax as a marketplace facilitator, at no charge to you, and notes that sellers with a physical presence in some states may still need to file a "zero dollar return."

Neither arrangement covers your own income tax. Talk to a tax professional about your situation.

File delivery and PDF protection

Gumroad. For products priced above $0.99, the largest single file is 20 GB and you can add as many files as you want. Free products are capped at 250 MB total. A "Download all" button appears only when total content is under 500 MB; Gumroad suggests uploading a ZIP otherwise. Its product editor lets you add files, stamped PDFs, pages and folders to organize what buyers see.

Payhip. Payhip allows files up to 5 GB each. After checkout, buyers are sent to a download page and emailed a receipt with the link. For protection, Payhip offers PDF stamping and adjustable download limits; stamping adds the buyer's email and purchase date to each page, for portrait PDFs under 250 MB.

For a typical printable PDF, both limits are far more than enough.

Who each platform tends to fit

Gumroad may fit if you:

  • Want a chance at marketplace discovery through Discover and accept the 30% rate on those sales.
  • Sell higher-priced products, where the fixed $0.50 matters less.
  • Want one company acting as Merchant of Record for every sale.
  • Are comfortable with a $100 payout minimum and scheduled payouts.

Payhip may fit if you:

  • Sell low-priced printables, where a percentage-only fee keeps more of each sale.
  • Drive your own traffic from a blog, email list or Pinterest.
  • Want each sale deposited to PayPal or Stripe right away.
  • Want the option to move to a flat monthly plan later.

Quick checklist before you choose

  • Calculate fees at your real price points, including processing.
  • Estimate how many sales a month you'll make and how that affects payout timing.
  • Check whether you'd rely on marketplace discovery or your own links.
  • Read each platform's current fee, payout and tax pages.
  • Track gross sales, fees and net each month. A simple sheet like the Calm Money Monthly Planner can help keep that in one place.

The right platform is the one whose fee structure and payout rhythm match how you price and promote your products. Do the math on your own catalog before you commit.
