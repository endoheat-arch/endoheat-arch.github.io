Payhip Memberships Explained: Recurring Access vs One-Time Downloads for Digital Sellers

If you sell printables or planners, most of your catalog is probably one-time digital downloads: the buyer pays once, gets the files, and keeps them. Payhip also supports a different product type called a membership, plus recurring pricing on bundles, courses and coaching. This explainer sticks to what Payhip’s Help Center documents about memberships, access and billing. It is general education for sellers, not financial, legal or tax advice, and not a promise of income.

What a Payhip membership product is

On Payhip, a membership is its own product type. Members get a dedicated portal where you publish posts—text, streamable video, images, links, polls, or embeds from other sites (Payhip Help: Add Membership Products). Create it from Products → Add new product → Membership, then manage posts from the Posts tab (Payhip Help: Add Membership Products).

You can brand the member area (banner, colors, fonts, headline), edit the sales and thank-you pages, and set Draft or Published (Payhip Help: Add Membership Products). You can also attach downloadable files to posts, up to 2GB (Payhip Help: Create a Membership Site on Payhip). Unlike a single PDF download, a membership is an ongoing feed of posts inside a member area.

Membership vs one-time digital downloads

A classic digital download is buy-once: the customer pays, receives the files, and keeps them. A membership is oriented around continued access to a portal and to new posts over time (Payhip Help: Create a Membership Site on Payhip). Payhip’s guide lists features that one-time downloads do not use the same way: schedule content ahead of time, automatically notify members about new posts, allow comments, and give different content by pricing tier (Payhip Help: Create a Membership Site on Payhip).

If the product is a finished planner pack with no monthly updates, a one-time download is usually simpler. If you will release new worksheets or short lessons on a cadence and want a place members return to, a membership fits better.

Membership vs product bundles

Payhip documents two ways to run membership-style access: membership products and product bundles (Payhip Help: Create a Membership Site on Payhip).

  • Membership products start with a dedicated portal of posts (text, video, image, link, poll, embed), with optional file attachments on those posts (Payhip Help: Create a Membership Site on Payhip; Payhip Help: Add Membership Products).
  • Product bundles package other store products—digital downloads, courses and even memberships—into one offer, with one-time or recurring pricing (Payhip Help: Product Bundles). Payhip’s example of an “all-access” style offer is a bundle that includes many store products on a subscription plan (Payhip Help: Product Bundles).

Payhip’s rule of thumb: use a bundle for access to a group of products already on your store; use a membership product for scheduling, member notifications, comments and tiered post access (Payhip Help: Create a Membership Site on Payhip). In both cases, canceling a subscription automatically ends access to the included content (Payhip Help: Create a Membership Site on Payhip).

One caveat: a 100% off coupon for other store products given to members is not tied to the subscription—if they cancel, they still keep those extra products (Payhip Help: Create a Membership Site on Payhip). Bundles handle multi-product access more cleanly when that is the goal.

How pricing and recurring billing work

On a membership (and on bundles), you can add multiple pricing plans: one-time payments, recurring subscriptions, a fixed number of months of charges, or a free plan (Payhip Help: Add Membership Products; Payhip Help: Product Bundles).

Recurring payments on Payhip are limited to bundles, memberships, courses and coaching products, and they require Stripe as the payment processor (Payhip Help: Set up Recurring Payments). Stripe connects from Account → Settings → Payment Details (Payhip Help: Connect Your Stripe Account).

For a subscription plan you set the amount and frequency: weekly, monthly, quarterly or annually (Payhip Help: Set up Recurring Payments). You can add a free trial measured in days, and optionally a custom first payment—Payhip requires that custom first amount to be greater than the default subscription price (Payhip Help: Set up Recurring Payments).

Payhip also supports a payment plan (a set number of monthly installments). Customers on a payment plan cannot cancel mid-plan and, after finishing payments, get lifetime access unless you manage access manually later (Payhip Help: Set up Recurring Payments). That is different from an open-ended monthly membership.

Cancel, failed payments and changing prices

Before you launch, note Payhip’s documented edge cases:

  • Cancel: access ends immediately; Payhip does not prorate remaining paid days (Payhip Help: Set up Recurring Payments).
  • Failed payment: automatic retries; three emails over 20 days; then the subscription is cancelled if unpaid (Payhip Help: Set up Recurring Payments).
  • Price changes: existing customers keep the price they signed up for until they cancel. Hide the old plan (“Delete plan”) and create a new one for future buyers; that does not cancel current subscribers (Payhip Help: Set up Recurring Payments).

Word your sales page so buyers understand cancel behavior and what they keep if they also bought one-time downloads outside the membership.

When a printable or planner seller should use which option

Use a one-time digital download for finished content—a budget planner PDF, checklist or printable pack. Buyers expect to pay once and keep the files. The Calm Money Monthly Budget Planner fits that pattern: useful on its own without a member portal.

Use a product bundle (one-time or subscription) when one checkout should unlock several existing store products, such as a printable library pass (Payhip Help: Product Bundles; Payhip Help: Create a Membership Site on Payhip).

Use a membership product when the value is ongoing posts—new templates, short videos, polls and scheduled drops in a branded member area, with optional tiers (Payhip Help: Create a Membership Site on Payhip). That only works if you can publish on a schedule members will notice.

A practical sequence: start with one-time downloads, add a bundle once you have related products, then open a membership only if you have a real content cadence. Payhip suggests creating test products in a free account to see which type fits (Payhip Help: Create a Membership Site on Payhip).

Quick setup checklist (from Payhip’s own flow)

1. Connect Stripe for recurring billing (Payhip Help: Set up Recurring Payments; Payhip Help: Connect Your Stripe Account). 2. Choose membership portal vs bundle of existing products (Payhip Help: Create a Membership Site on Payhip). 3. Add the product and pricing plans (trial or payment plan if needed) (Payhip Help: Add Membership Products; Payhip Help: Set up Recurring Payments). 4. Write the sales page with access rules, cancel behavior and what is included (Payhip Help: Add Membership Products). 5. Put a clear Log In button on the thank-you page, then publish when the first posts or included products are ready (Payhip Help: Add Membership Products).

The bottom line

A Payhip membership is a member portal fed by posts, with optional tiers, scheduling and notifications. A bundle fits “access to these store products.” One-time downloads remain simplest for finished printables. Recurring billing needs Stripe, supports weekly through annual cycles, and follows Payhip’s rules on cancel, failed payments and locked-in plan prices. Read the current Help articles before you launch—this is a product-design choice, not a guaranteed revenue stream.
