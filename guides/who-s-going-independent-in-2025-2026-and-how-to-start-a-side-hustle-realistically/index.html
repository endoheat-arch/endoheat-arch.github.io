Who's Going Independent in 2025-2026, and How to Start a Side Hustle Realistically

Intro: big trend, small beginnings

Independent work is no longer a fringe choice. MBO Partners' 2025 State of Independence study counts 72.9 million independent workers in the U.S., up slightly from 72.7 million in 2024 [MBO Partners, 2025]. At the same time, most side hustles are modest. Bankrate's 2025 survey found a median side hustle income of $200 a month [Bankrate, 2025].

Both things are true at once, and that is the point of this post: lots of people are working for themselves, but the realistic way in is small, specific and patient. Below is who is doing it, what they do, and simple first steps for the most common types of side work. Nothing here is a promise of income or personal financial advice.

Who independent workers are

MBO Partners defines independents broadly, covering full-time, part-time and occasional independent work. A few findings from its 2025 report stand out [MBO Partners, 2025]:

  • Most chose it. 63% say being independent is fully their choice.
  • It skews younger. Gen Z now makes up 28% of the independent workforce.
  • AI is already normal. 74% of independents use AI, and 61% say it saves time and increases output.
  • Platforms matter. 42% rely on digital platforms to find work.
  • High earners are growing, but they're the minority. A record 5.6 million independents earn more than $100,000 a year, up from 4.7 million in 2024 (MBO calls it a nearly 19% increase) and almost double the 3 million in 2020.

Put that last number in context: 5.6 million out of 72.9 million is well under one in ten. Six figures is possible, but it's not the typical outcome, especially in year one.

Bankrate's numbers point the same way on age: 34% of Gen Z adults have a side hustle, versus 31% of millennials, 23% of Gen X and 22% of boomers [Bankrate, 2025].

What side hustlers actually do

According to Bankrate, the most popular named categories are online sales (15% of side hustlers) and professional or business services such as freelance writing and consulting (14%). Next come food delivery and crafts (9% each), pet care (7%) and teaching or tutoring (6%) [Bankrate, 2025]. Notably, 43% said they work in a category Bankrate didn't list, which shows how varied this work is.

And it's often about more than extra spending money. 41% of side hustlers use some of the income for discretionary purchases, but 35% use at least some of it for regular living expenses, and 16% want their side hustle to become their main income [Bankrate, 2025].

First steps: freelancing in writing, design or virtual assistance

These overlap with Bankrate's "professional and business services" category and are one of the easiest places to start with skills you already have.

  • Pick one service and one type of client. "Blog editing for therapists" is easier to sell than "writing."
  • Make two or three samples. If you have no clients yet, make example pieces you'd be proud to show.
  • Write down a simple process. Agree on scope, deadline, number of revisions and payment terms before you start.
  • Use AI as a helper, not the product. Clients increasingly expect it: Upwork's In-Demand Skills 2026 report found AI image generation and editing grew 95% year over year in freelancer earnings [Upwork, 2026]. Be open about which tools you use and check every output yourself.

First steps: selling online

Online sales is the single most popular named category in Bankrate's survey [Bankrate, 2025]. It covers reselling, handmade goods and print-on-demand.

  • Test before you stock up. List a few items, or a few print-on-demand designs, before buying inventory.
  • Learn each marketplace's fees and rules before you set prices, since they vary a lot between platforms.
  • Track every sale and cost from day one, even in a basic spreadsheet. You'll need the numbers later.

First steps: professional services

If you have years of experience in a field such as bookkeeping, HR, marketing or project management, consulting can be a natural extension of it.

  • Start with your network. Former colleagues and clients already know your work.
  • Sell a defined package (for example, an audit or a 30-day setup) instead of open-ended hours.
  • Check your day job's rules on outside work and conflicts of interest.
  • Stay inside your credentials. Don't offer legal, tax, medical or investment advice unless you are licensed to.

First steps: digital products

Digital products (printables, templates, guides) aren't a named Bankrate category, but they overlap with online sales. They take effort up front, then each copy can be delivered automatically without inventory or shipping.

  • Solve one narrow problem for one specific group.
  • Make a small first version and improve it from buyer feedback instead of polishing for months.
  • Plan for discovery. A product nobody finds doesn't sell, so budget time for Pinterest, a blog or an email list.

First steps: local services

Food delivery, pet care and tutoring together cover a big share of Bankrate's named categories [Bankrate, 2025]. Local work is often the fastest to start because demand already exists nearby.

  • Start with people who know you: neighbors, community groups and local social media.
  • Check what's required locally, such as licenses, insurance or background checks, especially for pet care and work with children.
  • Ask happy customers for reviews. For local services, word of mouth often matters most.

A note on money and taxes

Side income is still income. In the U.S., self-employment tax is 15.3% (12.4% Social Security plus 2.9% Medicare), and the IRS says it generally applies once net self-employment earnings reach $400 [IRS]. Rules depend on your situation, so check the IRS page or a tax professional.

Conclusion

Independent work is big and still growing. MBO counts 72.9 million independents, most of whom chose it, and a growing group earning six figures [MBO Partners, 2025]. But the typical side hustle starts at a couple of hundred dollars a month [Bankrate, 2025]. The realistic path is to pick one category that matches skills you already have, take one small first step this week, track what you earn and spend, and grow from evidence rather than hype.
