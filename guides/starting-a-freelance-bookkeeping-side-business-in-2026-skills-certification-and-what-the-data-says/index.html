Intro: why bookkeeping is a skill-based side business

Small businesses need accurate books every month, whether or not the owner enjoys doing them. That makes bookkeeping a practical skill-based side business for people who are organized, comfortable with numbers and good with software. It's recurring work by nature: transactions keep coming, and someone has to record and reconcile them.

This guide uses U.S. Bureau of Labor Statistics data and Intuit's own QuickBooks program pages to explain what the work involves, what the market looks like and how people commonly get credentialed. It's an overview, not a promise of income.

What bookkeepers actually do

The BLS describes bookkeeping, accounting and auditing clerks as workers who compute, classify and record data to help organizations keep complete and accurate financial records [BLS]. Typical duties include:

  • Using bookkeeping software, spreadsheets and databases
  • Posting financial transactions into accounting software
  • Recording costs (debits) and income (credits) to the right accounts
  • Producing reports such as balance sheets and income statements
  • Checking figures, postings and reports for accuracy
  • Reconciling records and reporting any differences

[BLS]

Bookkeepers may also handle payroll, billing, purchasing and tracking overdue bills, and many of these tasks involve talking with clients [BLS]. The BLS notes that full-charge bookkeepers maintain an organization's entire set of books, and that bookkeepers who work for multiple firms may visit their clients' places of business [BLS].

What the 2025 pay data shows (and what it doesn't)

According to the BLS, the median annual wage for bookkeeping, accounting and auditing clerks was $50,670 in May 2025, or $24.36 an hour [BLS]. The lowest 10% earned less than $36,000 and the highest 10% earned more than $74,550 [BLS]. Median pay varied by industry, from $54,890 in construction to $45,830 in retail trade [BLS].

Two caveats matter for a side business:

  • These are wage figures for employees. BLS says its pay data doesn't include self-employed workers [BLS]. What a freelancer charges and keeps depends on rates, clients and hours, and it isn't captured here.
  • The data is a benchmark, not a forecast. Use it to judge what the skill is worth to employers, not to predict your own income.

The honest outlook: automation and the advisory shift

The BLS projects employment in this occupation to decline 6% from 2025 to 2035, compared with 3% growth across all occupations [BLS]. It says software has automated many routine tasks, so the same work can be done with fewer people [BLS].

The same projection also has two encouraging details:

  • There are still openings. BLS projects about 144,100 openings a year on average over the decade, all from replacing workers who change occupations or retire [BLS].
  • The role is shifting toward analysis. BLS expects these workers to take on a more analytical and advisory role, focusing less on manual data entry and more on analyzing clients' books and pointing out ways to be more efficient [BLS].

For a freelancer, the practical lesson is to sell more than data entry. Clean, reconciled books plus a clear monthly summary of what the numbers mean is the kind of work automation doesn't fully replace.

Getting credible: free QuickBooks certification

BLS says certification usually isn't required but can help show competency [BLS]. For small-business clients, software skills are often the first thing they ask about, and Intuit's QuickBooks program is a widely recognized option.

According to Intuit:

  • QuickBooks Online certification trainings and exams are free for accounting pros and available inside QuickBooks Online Accountant [Intuit, Training & certification].
  • Signing up for QuickBooks Online Accountant automatically unlocks ProAdvisor benefits [Intuit, Training & certification].
  • Training is optional, so you can go straight to the certification exam. If you don't pass after the third attempt, you're locked out for 30 days [Intuit, ProAdvisor FAQ].
  • You need to pass QuickBooks Online Certification Level 1 first. Intuit says it's best to wait at least 90 days before taking Level 2 [Intuit, ProAdvisor FAQ].
  • Recertification for Level 1 and Level 2 is offered annually, every May and June [Intuit, ProAdvisor FAQ].

Intuit also says ProAdvisors earn points toward program tiers through activities such as earning certifications and onboarding clients [Intuit, ProAdvisor Academy].

Know the line: bookkeeping vs tax preparation

Many bookkeeping clients will eventually ask, "Can you do my taxes too?" Know the rules before you say yes. The IRS says anyone who prepares or assists in preparing federal tax returns for compensation must have a valid 2026 PTIN (Preparer Tax Identification Number) before preparing returns [IRS, PTIN requirements].

Keeping the books and handing organized records to the client's tax professional is a different service from preparing returns. If you want to offer tax prep, look into the IRS requirements first. If not, say so clearly in your service agreement.

Services you can package

Based on the duties BLS describes [BLS], common freelance packages include:

  • Monthly bookkeeping: categorize transactions, reconcile bank and card accounts, deliver a profit and loss statement and balance sheet.
  • Cleanup or catch-up: bring months of unreconciled books up to date.
  • Accounts receivable support: send invoices and track overdue accounts.
  • Accounts payable support: record bills and keep a payment schedule.
  • Software setup: set up a chart of accounts and bank feeds in QuickBooks Online for a new business.
  • Monthly review call: walk the owner through the numbers. This leans into the advisory role BLS expects to grow [BLS].

Flat monthly packages with a clear transaction limit are easier for clients to budget for than open-ended hourly billing, and they make your own workload predictable.

A step-by-step starting plan

  • Learn the fundamentals. Make sure you're solid on debits and credits, reconciliations and the main financial statements.
  • Get software credentials. Work toward the free QuickBooks Online certification in QuickBooks Online Accountant [Intuit, Training & certification].
  • Practice on real books. Start with your own side-business records, or a friend's small business, with their permission.
  • Pick a niche. Construction had the highest median pay among the top industries BLS lists [BLS]. Even if you choose a different niche, knowing one industry's needs makes your marketing clearer.
  • Write a simple service agreement. Cover scope, monthly deadlines, what records the client must provide, confidentiality, and the fact that you don't prepare tax returns (unless you're qualified to).
  • Protect client data. BLS lists integrity and confidentiality as core qualities for this work [BLS]. Use secure document sharing and strong passwords.
  • Start small. One or two monthly clients will show you how much time the work really takes before you set your prices higher or take on more.

Conclusion

Freelance bookkeeping is a practical, skill-based side business with recurring demand, a free and recognized software credential, and a clear path from data entry to more valuable advisory work. The honest picture includes a projected decline in employee roles as automation spreads [BLS], which is why the best positioning is accurate books plus clear explanations. Start with the fundamentals, earn the certification, define your services and stay on the right side of the tax-preparation line.
