Intro: turning printable designs into print-on-demand books

If you already design planners, logbooks or journals as printables, Amazon's Kindle Direct Publishing (KDP) offers another way to sell them: as printed paperbacks. KDP prints books on demand and subtracts printing costs from your royalties, so you don't pay upfront or hold inventory [KDP, Printing cost].

This guide covers what KDP's own help pages say about low-content books, ISBNs, royalties and AI disclosure. It isn't a promise of sales; Amazon is a crowded marketplace, and results vary widely.

What KDP counts as a low-content book

KDP defines a low-content book as one with minimal or no content on the interior pages. These books are generally repetitive and designed to be filled in by the user [KDP, Low-content books]. Think planners, journals, logbooks and notebooks.

KDP says this doesn't usually include activity books such as puzzle or coloring books, since those generally don't repeat the same content on each page, though there can be exceptions [KDP, Low-content books].

ISBN options (and why they matter here)

Low-content books don't require an ISBN. You can publish without one or use your own, but low-content books aren't eligible for KDP's free ISBN [KDP, Low-content books]. KDP says eBooks and low-content books are the only formats where an ISBN is optional [KDP, ISBN and imprint].

The choice affects features:

  • Without an ISBN, the Read Sample feature and back-cover thumbnail aren't supported. KDP suggests buying your own ISBN from Bowker or your local ISBN agency if you want them, or using A+ Content to show interior images on the detail page [KDP, Low-content books].
  • With your own ISBN, you can list your own imprint (publisher name), and you must enter the same title, author and publisher details in KDP that you registered with the ISBN agency. A mismatch blocks publishing [KDP, ISBN and imprint].

KDP also notes that you can't change your ISBN choice after publishing without unpublishing and republishing the book [KDP, Low-content books].

How paperback royalties work after the 2025 change

KDP offers two paperback royalty rates on Amazon marketplaces, 60% or 50%, depending on list price. Since June 10, 2025, books priced below certain thresholds (for example, under $9.99 in the U.S.) earn 50% instead of 60% [KDP, Paperback royalty].

The formula is:

(Royalty rate x list price) – printing costs = royalty

KDP's own example: a $15 list price, 333-page regular-trim black-ink paperback sold in the U.S. earns (0.60 x $15) – $5.00 = $4.00 [KDP, Paperback royalty].

This matters for planners and journals, which are often priced low. At $8.99, the 50% rate applies before printing costs are taken out, so it's worth running the numbers in KDP's calculator before you settle on a price.

Printing costs and minimum list price

KDP calculates printing cost as a fixed cost plus page count times a per-page cost. The cost depends on page count, ink type (black or color), paper type and the marketplace where the book was ordered. Bleed settings and cover finish don't affect it [KDP, Printing cost]. For black-ink paperbacks with 24 to 110 pages, only the fixed cost applies (24 to 108 pages on Amazon.com.au) [KDP, Printing cost].

KDP also sets a minimum list price so your royalty always covers printing: printing cost divided by your royalty rate (50% or 60%). It won't let you enter a lower price [KDP, Printing cost]. KDP's Printing Cost & Royalty Calculator shows the exact figures [KDP, Printing cost].

AI disclosure rules

If you use AI, KDP's content guidelines require you to disclose AI-generated text, images or translations when you publish a new book or republish an edited one. That includes cover and interior images [KDP, Content guidelines].

KDP draws a line between two kinds of content:

  • AI-generated: content created by an AI tool counts as AI-generated even if you edited it heavily afterward. You must disclose it.
  • AI-assisted: if you created the content yourself and used AI to edit, refine, error-check or brainstorm, you don't need to disclose it.

[KDP, Content guidelines]

You're responsible for making sure all content, AI-generated or not, follows KDP's guidelines, including intellectual property rights [KDP, Content guidelines].

What low-content books can't do

Some KDP features aren't available for low-content books:

  • Expanded Distribution (selling through channels beyond Amazon) isn't supported, whatever ISBN option you choose [KDP, Low-content books].
  • Read Sample isn't available without an ISBN, as noted above [KDP, Low-content books].

For context, Expanded Distribution on regular paperbacks pays 40% of list price minus printing costs [KDP, Paperback royalty]. That isn't an option for low-content books, so plan around Amazon marketplace sales.

A simple launch checklist

  • Pick a specific niche. "Weekly meal planner for families" is clearer than "notebook."
  • Make the interior genuinely useful. Base it on a layout you'd use yourself.
  • Decide on an ISBN first. Publish without one, or buy your own if you want Read Sample and your own imprint.
  • Price with the calculator. Check whether your price falls in the 50% or 60% band, and what's left after printing.
  • Check the print layout. Review margins and page count against KDP's formatting requirements before you publish.
  • Disclose AI-generated content if you used AI to create text or images.
  • Show the interior. If you skip an ISBN, use A+ Content images so buyers can see the pages.

If you also sell printable versions, the print and digital formats can serve different buyers: some people want a bound book, others want to print pages as needed. For example, my Weekly Meal Planner is a printable PDF on Payhip.

Conclusion

KDP lets designers of planners and journals sell printed books with no inventory or upfront printing costs. The details matter, though: low-content books don't get a free ISBN or Expanded Distribution, books priced under the threshold earn 50% instead of 60%, and AI-generated content must be disclosed. Start with one well-made book, run the numbers in KDP's calculator, and check the current help pages before you publish, since terms can change.
