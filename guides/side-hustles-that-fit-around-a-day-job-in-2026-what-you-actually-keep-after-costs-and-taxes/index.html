Intro: gross pay is the wrong number to compare

Most side-hustle lists rank options by headline pay. But if you're fitting extra work around a full-time job, the number that matters is what's left after platform fees, work costs and taxes, divided by every hour you actually spend, including unpaid time.

This post looks at five low-to-medium-effort options and how to think about their net pay, using each source's own published figures. The math examples are illustrations, not tax advice or a promise of income. Your costs and tax situation will differ, so check with a tax professional for your own numbers.

What side hustlers really earn (Bankrate)

Bankrate's 2025 Side Hustle Survey of 2,616 U.S. adults (fieldwork June 2–4, 2025) found 27% have a side hustle, the lowest share since 2017 and down from 36% in 2024 [Bankrate, 2025]. Side hustlers earned a median of $200 a month and an average of $885 [Bankrate, 2025]. Bankrate also found that 28% of side hustlers make between $1 and $50 a month [Bankrate, 2025].

The gap between that median and average is a reminder: a few strong earners pull the average up. Plan around realistic numbers.

The three deductions from every side-hustle dollar

1. Platform fees. Fiverr says sellers earn 80% of each completed order [Fiverr, Earnings page]. Upwork's freelancer service fee ranges from 0% to 15% per contract [Upwork, Service fees].

2. Work costs. For driving gigs, the big one is your car. The IRS standard mileage rate for business use is 76 cents a mile for July 1 to Dec. 31, 2026 [IRS, Mileage rates]. That figure is a good reminder of what each mile really costs you, even if you never claim it.

3. Taxes. If you're self-employed, the IRS says self-employment tax is 15.3% (12.4% for Social Security and 2.9% for Medicare), on top of income tax [IRS, Self-employment tax]. And if your net self-employment earnings are $400 or more in a year, you have to file a federal return [IRS, Self-employed tax center].

Option 1: Delivery blocks (Amazon Flex)

Effort: low skill, schedulable. Main cost: your vehicle.

Amazon says most Flex drivers earn $18 to $25 an hour, and that actual earnings vary based on location, tips, delivery time and other factors [Amazon Flex]. You need to be 21 or older with a valid license, a qualifying vehicle and a smartphone, and you can choose delivery blocks up to a week in advance [Amazon Flex].

On tips, Amazon says that for tip-eligible deliveries, you keep 100% of tips on top of the earnings shown in the app [Amazon Flex, Earnings]. Its FAQ gives an example: if an offer shows $36–50 for a two-hour block, Amazon's guaranteed contribution is $36, and any tips are added on top [Amazon Flex, FAQ].

Net-pay check: subtract the miles you drive (including to the pickup station) and self-employment tax before comparing Flex with other options.

Option 2: Remote tech support skills

Effort: medium. Main cost: time to build skills.

If you're the person friends call when their laptop breaks, the employer market puts a value on that. According to BLS May 2025 wage data, computer user support specialists earned a median of $29.74 an hour ($61,860 a year), and computer network support specialists earned a median of $36.64 an hour [BLS, OEWS May 2025; BLS, Occupational Outlook Handbook].

These are employee wages, not freelance rates, but they're a useful benchmark when you price local or remote help for small businesses and households.

Option 3: Freelance writing, such as grant writing

Effort: medium. Main cost: platform fees and unbilled research time.

Upwork's rate page says grant writers typically charge $35 to $60 an hour, based on historical contracts worldwide. It gives tiers of $35 for beginners, $60 for intermediate writers and $125 for advanced writers [Upwork, Grant writers cost].

Net-pay check: after Upwork's service fee (0% to 15%) [Upwork, Service fees] and self-employment tax [IRS, Self-employment tax], your effective rate will be noticeably lower, and lower still once you count the hours spent on proposals and research.

Option 4: Single-day gigs on Fiverr

Effort: medium, with deadline pressure. Main cost: Fiverr's 20% cut.

A 2026 NetCredit study, covered by Forbes, looked at Fiverr prices from top-rated U.S. sellers offering 24-hour delivery. Six categories tied at a median of $100 for a 24-hour assignment: AI spokesperson videos, business planning, AI applications, applications and registration, marketing strategy and generative AI lessons [NetCredit, 2026; Forbes, Sept. 22, 2026].

Net-pay check: a $100 order leaves you $80 after Fiverr's cut [Fiverr, Earnings page], before taxes and any tool subscriptions. NetCredit notes that AI spokesperson video tools often need a paid subscription [NetCredit, 2026].

Option 5: AI-assisted creative work

Effort: medium. Main cost: tool subscriptions and learning time.

Upwork's In-Demand Skills 2026 report found AI video generation and editing grew 329% year over year, the fastest-growing skill in its Design & Creative category [Upwork, In-Demand Skills 2026]. Fast growth from a smaller base doesn't guarantee volume, but it does point to where demand is moving.

Net-pay check: count your monthly AI tool subscriptions as a fixed cost and spread them across the jobs you actually land.

A simple net-pay worksheet

[Suggested image: a simple table graphic showing gross, fees, costs, tax set-aside, net, hours, net per hour]

For each option, write down:

  • Gross earned for the week or month
  • Minus platform fees (for example, Fiverr's 20% or your Upwork fee)
  • Minus work costs (miles, tools, subscriptions, supplies)
  • Minus a tax set-aside (ask a tax pro what percentage fits your situation; self-employment tax alone is 15.3% [IRS, Self-employment tax])
  • = Net
  • Divide by total hours, including travel, admin, proposals and revisions

The result is your real hourly rate. Compare that number, not the headline rate, across options.

Conclusion

The best side hustle to fit around a day job isn't the one with the biggest headline number. It's the one with the highest net rate for the hours and energy you can realistically give it. Driving blocks are easy to schedule but carry vehicle costs. Skill-based work like tech support or writing pays more per hour but takes time to build. Single-day and AI-assisted gigs can pay well per job, but platform cuts and tool costs come out first. Run the worksheet for a month before you decide where to focus.
