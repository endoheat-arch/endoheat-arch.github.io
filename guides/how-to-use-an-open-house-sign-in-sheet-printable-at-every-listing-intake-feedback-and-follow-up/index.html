How to Use an Open House Sign In Sheet Printable at Every Listing (Intake, Feedback and Follow-Up)

Originally published on Telegraph

You have twenty minutes before the first door knock, and the clipboard still has last week's scrap paper taped over a half-erased address. Visitors will ask about price, schools and financing while you try to remember who already left a card. An open house sign in sheet printable will not sell the house for you, but it gives every visitor a clear place to leave contact details, agent status and how they heard about the listing — so you walk out with a real list instead of a pile of sticky notes.

1. Set up your open house sign in sheet printable before the first visitor

Spend about fifteen minutes the night before (or the morning of) so the door is ready when people arrive.

  • A sturdy clipboard or thin binder dedicated to this listing
  • Several copies of the sign-in sheet (standard for most events; large-print if you expect older visitors or want bigger writing space)
  • A fine-tip pen that works on the paper you printed, plus a spare
  • A discreet folder for completed sheets so names are not left face-up on the kitchen island
  • A small stack of your business cards next to the clipboard

Write the property address, date and your name at the top of each sheet before anyone walks in. That habit stops you from mixing Saturday's sign-ins with Sunday's tour of a different listing. Clip a clean sheet on top and keep extras behind it so you never run out mid-event.

If your brokerage requires a specific disclosure, QR code or privacy notice at the door, add that beside the clipboard — the printable is a contact log, not a substitute for required brokerage paperwork.

2. What belongs on an open house sign in sheet template

A good open house sign in sheet template asks for only what you will actually use in follow-up. Too many blank fields and people skip lines; too few and you cannot tell a serious buyer from a curious neighbor.

Useful fields on a clean sheet:

  • Name
  • Phone and email
  • Whether they are already working with an agent (yes/no)
  • Whether they are pre-approved (yes/no or a short note)
  • How they heard about the open house (sign, online listing, agent, neighbor, social)

The Keystone kit includes a standard sheet with those fields and a large-print version that asks for name, phone or email, and whether they came with an agent — useful when writing space or eyesight makes the full grid awkward.

Tip: Stand near the door for the first few minutes and greet people as they sign. A friendly "thanks — I'll follow up with any new details on the property" raises completion rates more than a passive clipboard in a dark corner.

After the open house, transfer hot leads into your CRM or follow-up log the same day. A signed sheet that sits in your car for a week is almost as useless as no sheet.

3. A realtor open house checklist for the day before and morning of

An open house checklist (realtor-facing, not a buyer tour list) keeps the event from becoming improvisation. Use a listing prep checklist in the days leading up to launch, then a short morning-of list you can scan in five minutes.

Day-before / listing-prep style items that matter:

  • Signed listing agreement and price confirmed with the seller
  • Photos, description and showing instructions already live
  • Lockbox, sign and access notes working
  • Repairs and decluttering done enough that feedback will focus on the house, not the mess
  • Disclosures and any required documents gathered per your brokerage
  • Open house date set and promoted the way your team usually promotes

Morning-of open house items:

  • Sign-in sheets printed and headed with address/date/agent
  • Lights on, music low or off per seller preference, temperature comfortable
  • Flyers or feature sheets stocked
  • Your phone charged; a note of the Wi-Fi (if you share it) or a plan if you do not
  • Plan for pets, parking and neighbor courtesy if the seller asked for it

You do not need a perfect staged magazine shoot. You do need a repeatable checklist so "I forgot the sign-in sheets" never happens on a busy Saturday.

4. Capture must-haves with a buyer intake form (real estate)

Open houses create names. First buyer meetings create plans. A buyer intake form real estate agents actually fill out turns "looking for something with a yard" into a page you can act on.

In the first sit-down (or a calm corner after an open house), capture:

  • Client name(s), phone, email and preferred contact method
  • Current housing (rent or own) and timeline to move
  • Price range and pre-approval status / lender if they have one
  • Target areas, bedrooms/bathrooms, must-haves and deal-breakers
  • Commute or school needs
  • Showing availability (days and times that actually work)

Read the answers back once: "So you're aiming for X area, under Y, by Z month, and a home office is a must — did I get that right?" That thirty-second check saves wasted showings.

Keep one intake form per buyer household in a client folder. When they pivot from condos to townhomes, update the same page instead of starting a new email thread you will never find again.

5. Use a seller intake form before you price and promote

Sellers need a parallel page. A seller intake form captures why they are moving, ideal timeline, recent updates, known issues, mortgage or lien notes they volunteer, price expectations, showing instructions, pets/access and what they plan next.

You are not practicing law or underwriting on this page — you are organizing what the seller told you so listing prep, showings and later negotiations stay consistent. Anything that must live on required disclosure forms still belongs on those forms per your state and brokerage.

6. Collect a showing feedback form after every tour

Sellers ask "What did people think?" A blank stare helps no one. A showing feedback form gives you a structured way to rate and comment after each showing or open-house wave.

Useful prompts:

  • Overall impression (1–5) plus a short comment
  • Price vs. value, condition, layout/flow and curb appeal
  • Likelihood of an offer
  • What buyers liked most
  • Concerns or suggestions

Fill it out while you still remember the conversation — in the car before the next appointment is ideal. Then share the pattern with the seller (with any privacy limits your brokerage requires). Three forms that all say "kitchen feels dark" are more useful than one vague "people liked it."

Pair feedback with a showing schedule page when you run a full day of tours: time, address, listing agent/access, price and a quick notes/rating column. One glance tells you which properties still need follow-up calls.

7. Keep listing prep and closing checklists on paper you can see

Digital task apps are fine until you are standing in a hallway with a dead phone battery. Paper checklists for listing prep and closing keep milestones visible:

Listing prep examples: signed agreement, comps reviewed, price set, repairs and clean, photos, measurements, disclosures, description, lockbox and sign, showing instructions, open house date, listing live, feedback follow-up plan.

Closing examples: offer accepted, earnest money, inspection and response, appraisal, loan commitment, title, HOA docs if any, repairs verified, utilities, final walkthrough, closing time/place, keys and codes handed over, thank-you / review request.

Check items off the day they happen. When a client asks "Where are we?" you point at the page instead of scrolling three apps.

8. Log follow-ups so open-house leads do not go cold

The client follow-up log is the quiet page that earns its keep after the balloons come down. For each touch, write:

  • Date
  • Client name
  • Method (call, text, email, in person)
  • Topic (new listing match, price update, closing thank-you, annual check-in)
  • Next step and date

After an open house, schedule the first follow-up within the window your brokerage and local norms allow — often within a day for people who asked a detailed question or left pre-approval notes. For past clients, a light annual or seasonal note keeps the relationship warm without spam.
