1. Gather the details before you print any baby shower invitations printable

The fastest way to waste cardstock is to print before the details are final. Before you print, write these on one sticky note and check them with the parent-to-be (or whoever is co-hosting):

  • Who it honors. The name as the parent-to-be likes it written. If two parents are being celebrated, include both names.
  • Date and time. Include an end time. People plan around it, especially guests with kids or a long drive.
  • Place. Full address, plus anything a first-time visitor needs to know: which building, where to park, whether the doorbell works.
  • RSVP. One name and one contact method, plus a reply-by date.
  • Registry or gift ideas. Where the registry lives, or a short note if the parent-to-be prefers something else.
  • Anything special. A theme, a "bring a book instead of a card" request, a diaper raffle, a sprinkle for a second baby, or a virtual option for faraway guests.

Once the note is final, copy it onto every card. If something changes later, you only have to fix it in one place before sending a quick reminder.

2. What to put on a baby shower invitation (and what to leave off)

People search what to put on a baby shower invitation because there's a small worry about forgetting something important. Here's the short version, in the order most cards use:

  • A headline. "Baby Shower," "A Little One Is on the Way," or "Please Join Us."
  • The honoree. "Honoring Maya" or "Honoring Sam and Jordan."
  • Date, time and place. Written clearly, with the weekday so nobody mixes up the date.
  • Hosted by. Optional, but helpful when guests don't know the host. A single line on the Note works: "Hosted by Aunt Lee and Priya."
  • RSVP to, and by when. A name, a phone number or email, and a date.
  • Registry or gift ideas. One line pointing guests to the registry.
  • A short note. Theme, dress code, parking, or "kids welcome."

What to leave off: long paragraphs, a complete schedule of games, and anything that sounds like a demand. If the card feels crowded, move the extra details to a follow-up text or a small insert card.

What name do you put on a baby shower invitation? Use the name the parent-to-be actually goes by. If the baby's name has been shared and the parents are happy for it to appear, you can add it ("Welcoming baby Theo"). If they haven't shared it, leave it off. That decision belongs to them.

3. How to fill out baby shower invitations by hand

How to fill out baby shower invitations neatly mostly comes down to setup. A few habits make handwritten cards look intentional rather than rushed:

  • Print on cardstock. Regular printer paper works in a pinch, but heavier paper feels like an invitation and survives the mail. Check your printer's settings for a cardstock or heavy-paper option.
  • Do a test page first. Print one sheet, check the colors and margins, then print the rest.
  • Fill in before you cut. Write on the full sheet while it lies flat, let the ink dry, then cut along the borders. A paper trimmer gives straighter edges than scissors.
  • Use one pen. A fine-tip felt pen in black or a dark color matching the design keeps every card consistent.
  • Write the shared details first. Fill in date, time, place and RSVP on every card in one pass, then go back and add the personal notes.
  • Keep a master copy. Fill out one card completely and set it aside as your reference so the details stay the same on every card.

If your handwriting makes you nervous, print the headline, then write only the short blanks. Short entries such as "Sat Nov 14, 2 to 4 pm" look tidy in almost any handwriting.

4. Baby shower invitation wording you can copy

Here are some baby shower invitation wording ideas that fit on a fill-in card. Swap in your own names and details.

Classic:

  • "Please join us for a baby shower honoring Maya. Hope you can make it!"

For both parents:

  • "Join us to celebrate Sam and Jordan as they get ready to welcome their little one."

Hosted by grandparents or family:

  • "Grandma Rose and Grandpa Ed invite you to a baby shower for Maya."

Casual or coed:

  • "Snacks, games and good company to celebrate Maya and Chris. Partners and kids welcome."

For a sprinkle (a smaller shower for a second or later baby):

  • "A little sprinkle for baby number two. Come celebrate Maya with us!"

For a virtual or hybrid shower:

  • "Join us in person or by video. The link is coming by text the week before."

Keep the Note line short. One friendly sentence beats three explanatory ones.

5. Baby shower invitation wording with the baby registry, diaper raffle and unwrapped gifts

This is where hosts tend to overthink. Gift notes on a baby shower invitation are common, and guests usually appreciate knowing where to look. The trick is to keep them short and optional-sounding.

Baby shower invitation wording with baby registry:

  • "Registered at [store or site] under Maya Lopez."
  • "Gift ideas: Maya is registered online. Search her name, or ask me for the link."

Diaper raffle:

  • "Bring a pack of diapers (any size) for a chance to win a prize at the shower!"

Books instead of a card:

  • "Instead of a card, please bring a favorite children's book with a note inside."

Not wrapping gifts:

  • "To keep things simple and green, feel free to bring gifts unwrapped."

No gifts:

  • "Your company is the only gift needed."

On the Confetti set, the Baby Shower card has its own Registry / gift ideas line, so the gift note doesn't need to squeeze into the general Note line. If you have more than one gift instruction, put the most important one on the card and save the second for a reminder text.

6. When to send baby shower invitations (a simple timeline)

There's no official rule, but a common approach for when to send baby shower invitations is about four to six weeks before the shower. That gives guests time to plan and shop without forgetting. Mailed cards need a few extra days for delivery, and guests who travel may appreciate a save-the-date even earlier.

A timeline that works for most home-hosted showers:

  • 6 to 8 weeks out: Confirm the date, place and guest list with the parent-to-be. Ask whether the registry is ready.
  • 5 to 6 weeks out: Print, fill in and address the invitations. Send save-the-dates to anyone traveling.
  • 4 to 5 weeks out: Mail or hand out the invitations.
  • About 2 weeks out: RSVP deadline. Choose a reply-by date that leaves you time to finalize food and seating.
  • A few days before: Text a friendly reminder with parking or entry details.
  • Within a couple of weeks after: Help the parent-to-be send thank-you notes.

If the shower is coming up fast, don't worry. A printed fill-in invitation handed out a couple of weeks ahead, followed by a text, still works fine.

7. Track RSVPs on one sheet

Invitations are the easy part. Keeping track of who's coming is where it gets messy. Use one list, either on paper or in a notes app, with four columns:

  • Name (and how many in their party)
  • Invited (date sent)
  • Reply (yes, no or maybe)
  • Notes (allergies, bringing a dish, needs a ride)

If guests prefer replying by mail, the RSVP reply cards in the Confetti set give them a simple "Kindly Reply" card to send back. Two days after the reply-by date, send a short text to anyone you haven't heard from: "Hi! Just checking if you can make Maya's shower on Saturday. No pressure either way."

8. Finish with matching favor tags and thank-you notes

Small matching touches make a homemade shower feel put together:

  • Favor tags. Tie a "Thank you for coming!" tag to a small treat bag or a seed packet.
  • Food labels. Use the blank "Name / label" tags on the dessert or snack table, and note common allergens if you know them.
  • Name tags. The same blank labels work as name tags when the two families haven't met yet.
  • Thank-you notes. Keep a stack of printed thank-you cards and a pen next to the gift table. A helper can jot down who brought what as gifts are opened, which makes writing notes afterward much faster.

Common baby shower invitation questions

Can I just text or email the invitation? Of course. Many hosts send a printed card to grandparents and older relatives, and text a photo of the same card to everyone else, so the details match everywhere.

How many invitations should I print? Count your guest list, then print a few extra for mistakes and late additions. With a printable set, running short just means printing another sheet.

Should the parent-to-be see the invitation first? Unless it's a surprise shower, yes. A quick photo of the master card lets them catch a misspelled name or a wrong date before everything goes out.
