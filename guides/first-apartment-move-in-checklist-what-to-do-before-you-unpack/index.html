First Apartment Move-In Checklist (What to Do Before You Unpack)

Originally published on Telegraph

Getting the keys feels huge. Then the boxes hit the floor and a dozen "I should have asked" moments show up. A first apartment move in checklist keeps the first week calm, protects your deposit, and gets utilities and budgets under control before the chaos wins. Whether you call it a new apartment move in checklist or an apartment move in checklist printable, the goal is the same: handle the process before you unpack.

# How to use a first apartment move in checklist before unpack day

1. Do a walkthrough before you unpack

Walk every room with your phone camera and a notepad. Note scratches, stains, missing blinds, sticky locks, and anything that doesn't work. Date the photos. Send a polite note to the landlord or property manager with the list so pre-existing damage isn't blamed on you later. This one step saves more deposit money than almost anything else on an apartment move in checklist landlord handoff.

2. Set up utilities and internet in the first 48 hours

Call (or use the provider apps) for electricity, gas if needed, water/trash if you pay them, and internet. Write down confirmation numbers, account numbers, and the start date for each service. Keep those details in one place so you are not digging through texts when something goes wrong. Setting up utilities for a new apartment early beats sitting in the dark on night one.

3. Change your address and key contacts

Update your bank, employer, school, insurance, subscriptions, and anyone who mails you. Forward mail for at least a few months. Add the landlord, building manager, and after-hours maintenance numbers to your phone the same day. A who-to-call sheet beats scrambling when a pipe drips at 11 PM.

4. Separate one-time costs from monthly rent

Move-in money and monthly money are different piles. One-time usually includes deposit, first month, maybe last month, application fees, truck or movers, basic furniture, and cleaning supplies. Monthly is rent, utilities, internet, renter's insurance, and groceries. Write both lists once so rent week does not surprise you.

5. Buy the boring essentials first

Before decorating, get a working smoke/CO situation checked, a basic toolkit, trash bags, toilet paper, a shower curtain, cleaning spray, light bulbs, and a power strip. A short essentials list beats three expensive Target runs. For the full what-to-buy list, see the related First Apartment Essentials Checklist Printable.

6. Give yourself a six-week runway when you can

If you still have time before move day, work backward: confirm the lease and keys, schedule the truck, measure the biggest furniture, pack a first-night box (sheets, chargers, meds, snacks), and leave buffer days for delays. Even two weeks of planning beats packing at midnight.

Move-in inspection checklist for your apartment (photos + landlord note)

A move in inspection checklist apartment walkthrough is not optional if you want your deposit back later. Treat it like a short form you fill with evidence:

  • Walk room by room: entry, living room, kitchen, bedrooms, bathroom(s), closets, balcony or patio if you have one
  • Photograph floors, walls, ceilings, appliances, windows, blinds, and fixtures — wide shots plus close-ups of any issue
  • Note the date and time on each photo or in a folder name
  • Write a short bullet list (room + issue + photo count)
  • Email or portal-message the landlord or property manager the same day with the list and photos attached
  • Keep a copy of that message in the same folder as your lease

A printed move in inspection form (or a blank sheet with room headers) helps you not skip closets and under-sink cabinets. If the building gives you an official form, fill that too — and still keep your own photos.

How to set up utilities for a new apartment

When you set up utilities new apartment accounts, do it in a fixed order so nothing falls through: 1. Confirm what you pay vs what the landlord pays (check the lease) 2. Call or open accounts for electric, gas (if any), water/trash (if tenant-paid), and internet 3. Ask for the earliest start date that covers move-in day or the day before 4. Write provider name, account number, confirmation number, deposit amount, and start date on one setup tracker page 5. Save login emails and passwords in a password manager, not on a sticky note in a moving box

Internet often takes the longest to schedule, so book that first. If you need a temporary hotspot for the first night, plan that before the truck arrives.

First night in a new apartment checklist

A first night in new apartment checklist is really a small "open first" box and a short list of non-negotiables:

  • Sheets, pillow, and a blanket (or an air mattress if the bed isn't set up)
  • Phone charger, night-light or lamp, and a power strip
  • Toilet paper, soap, toothbrush, meds, and a towel
  • Snacks, water, and something easy to eat without a full kitchen setup
  • Trash bags, paper towels, and basic cleaning spray
  • Lease packet, keys, fobs, and the who-to-call sheet on the counter
  • Confirm locks work and you know where the breaker box and water shutoff are

Unpacking can wait. Sleep, safety, and one clean bathroom matter more than hanging art.

What to do when you move into a new apartment (first week priorities)

If you're asking what to do when you move into a new apartment, use the first week for systems, not decoration:

  • Finish the inspection note and confirm the landlord received it
  • Confirm every utility and internet account is live and billed to the right address
  • Finish address changes and mail forwarding
  • Buy renter's insurance if your lease requires it (or you want the coverage)
  • Set your monthly budget with the real rent and utility estimates
  • Locate trash/recycling rules, package delivery spots, and parking rules
  • Test smoke and CO detectors; replace batteries if needed
  • Meet a neighbor or the building manager once so a face goes with the name

Save deep decorating and furniture hunting for week two once the boring systems are running.
