How to Bundle Digital Products on Payhip: Packages, Pricing Plans and When to Use a Membership Instead

If you already sell a few digital downloads — planners, checklists, wall art files — selling them only one at a time can make buyers click through checkout three times for a set they already want. Payhip's Product Bundles feature packages courses, digital products and memberships into one checkout, with optional one-time or recurring pricing.

Everything below comes from Payhip's Help Center (linked in Sources). Features change, so open those pages when you build yours.

General information only — not financial, legal or tax advice, and not a promise of income.

Why bundles matter when you sell more than one printable

Payhip's example is clear: three courses at $49 each can become one package at a price such as $119 (Product Bundles). The same pattern works for printables. Keep each file as its own product so solo buyers still have a path, then add a bundle that enrolls someone in several products at once.

Payhip also describes an all-access option: put many store products in a bundle and attach a subscription plan for catalog access (Product Bundles). That is optional; many printable shops start with a simple one-time starter kit and stop there.

What a Payhip bundle can include

According to Payhip, a bundle can be made of:

  • Digital products only
  • Courses only
  • Memberships only
  • Or a mix of those types

It is described as completely flexible within those product types (Product Bundles). Payhip's membership comparison article adds that bundles enroll subscribers in all included products through a single checkout (Create a Membership Site on Payhip).

That is different from discounting three separate listings by hand. The buyer pays once; Payhip enrolls them in each included product.

Step-by-step: create and publish a bundle

Payhip's setup steps are short (Product Bundles):

1. Open your Products page and click Add new product 2. Choose the Bundle option 3. Enter a name and click Add Bundle 4. On the Bundle Products page, use Add Product to pick which existing products belong in the package 5. Under the Pricing tab, add one or more pricing plans 6. Under Pages, edit the Sales Page so shoppers see what is included and why 7. On the Publish tab, switch from Draft to Published and save

Two pages deserve extra care. The sales page is "the face of your bundle," in Payhip's words — it should explain what buyers get and why they should enroll (Product Bundles). The thank-you page is where buyers land after checkout; Payhip says it must show a prominent Log In button, because the first thing they need is access to their account (Product Bundles).

Pricing plans: one-time, subscription, installments or free

On a bundle's Pricing tab, Payhip lets you add as many plans as you want. Supported plan styles include (Product Bundles; Set up Recurring Payments):

  • One-time payment
  • Recurring subscription (weekly, monthly, quarterly or annually)
  • Payment plan with a set number of monthly charges
  • An entirely free plan

Recurring payments are not available on every Payhip product type. Payhip states that recurring is currently limited to bundles, memberships, courses and coaching, and only when Stripe is enabled as the payment processor (Set up Recurring Payments).

For subscriptions, you can set a free trial in days, and optionally a custom first-period price — that first amount must be greater than the default subscription price (Set up Recurring Payments).

Payhip also notes that existing subscribers keep the price they signed up for until they cancel; hiding ("deleting") a plan does not cancel them. Failed payments start an automatic retry path with customer emails over about 20 days, then cancel if unpaid. Subscription cancellation ends access immediately with no proration. Payment-plan buyers cannot cancel mid-plan and keep lifetime access after finishing payments unless you unenroll them (Set up Recurring Payments).

None of that is a recommendation to use subscriptions. For many printable sellers, a clear one-time bundle price is enough.

Bundle vs membership product — which fits printable sellers

Payhip offers two paths for "membership-like" access: a membership product and a product bundle. Their comparison article is the place to decide (Create a Membership Site on Payhip).

Use a bundle when you mainly want to sell a group of products already listed in your store (PDFs, courses, even memberships) as one package. New content added to a bundle reaches existing buyers within about one hour, per Payhip (Create a Membership Site on Payhip; Product Bundles).

Use a membership product when you want a portal of posts (text, video, images, links, polls, embeds), scheduling, new-content notifications, comments and tier-based access — with optional downloads on posts up to 2GB (Create a Membership Site on Payhip).

In both cases, cancelling a subscription ends access to included content (Create a Membership Site on Payhip). For a shop built around downloadable planners, a bundle of existing digital products is usually the closer fit.

After the sale: access when you change the bundle

Payhip documents how access changes when you edit a live bundle (Product Bundles):

  • Add products later → new buyers get everything immediately; existing buyers get the new items after about a 1 hour delay
  • Remove products later → future buyers do not get them; existing buyers keep those products unless you unenroll them from the specific products
  • Unenrolling from the bundle only blocks future products you add; items already in the buyer's account stay until you unenroll those products one by one

Manage that under the bundle's Members area (View Details → "..." → Unenroll). Say on the sales page what is included today and whether later additions are part of the deal.

A practical packing list for your first bundle

Before you click Published:

1. Sell (or at least draft) each file as its own digital product first, so the bundle has real products to enroll 2. Name the bundle for the outcome ("Calm Money Starter Kit"), not a vague "Bundle 1" 3. List exactly which files or products are inside on the sales page 4. Start with one clear one-time price unless you already use Stripe and want recurring 5. Edit the thank-you page so Log In is obvious (Product Bundles) 6. Test with a coupon or low-price plan so you can walk through checkout as a buyer

If you already sell something like the Calm Money Monthly Planner, a natural next listing is a small kit: that planner plus matching worksheets or trackers as a single Payhip bundle — same files people can still buy alone, plus a package price for the full set.

Bundles do not invent demand. They make it easier for someone who already likes your printables to take home more of them in one click, on terms Payhip has documented end to end.
