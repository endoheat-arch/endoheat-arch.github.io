Intro: a realistic look at side income

Side hustles are common, but they are not a guaranteed windfall. In Bankrate's 2025 survey, 27% of U.S. adults said they have a side hustle, down from 36% in 2024 and the lowest share since 2017 [Bankrate, 2025]. Side hustlers reported earning an average of $885 a month, but the median was just $200 a month [Bankrate, 2025]. That gap matters: a handful of high earners pull the average up, while a typical side hustle brings in a few hundred dollars or less.

So the useful question isn't "what pays the most?" It's "where is demand actually growing, and what can I offer that fits my skills and time?" Below are five areas with real, published data behind them, plus practical tips for each. None of these figures are promises of what you will earn.

Strategy 1: Add AI skills to work you already do

The clearest freelance trend right now is not "AI replacing freelancers" but freelancers using AI inside familiar work. Upwork's In-Demand Skills 2026 report found that skills explicitly referencing AI grew 109% year over year in freelancer earnings, versus 23% for other in-demand skills [Upwork, 2026]. The fastest-growing skill was AI video generation and editing, up 329%, followed by AI integration (+178%) and AI data annotation and labeling (+154%) [Upwork, 2026]. The data covers U.S.-originated contracted work on Upwork in 2025 compared with 2024.

Rates vary widely. Upwork's own cost guide lists AI developers at $30 to $150 an hour in median hourly rates from historical contracts [Upwork, AI Developers]. That range tells you specialization and track record matter a lot.

Practical tips:

  • Pair AI with a skill you already have (video editing, design, writing, support) rather than selling "AI" on its own.
  • Build two or three small portfolio samples that show a before-and-after result.
  • Be upfront with clients about which tools you use and what you review by hand.

Strategy 2: AI training and evaluation work

Companies building AI models need people to rate answers, compare outputs and check code. DataAnnotation is one platform that publishes its rates. Its FAQ lists general projects starting at $25 to $50+ an hour, and coding projects starting at $40 to $150+ an hour, with STEM and professional (law, finance, medicine) tracks starting at $40 to $125+ [DataAnnotation FAQ]. Elsewhere on the same page it describes general work as $25 to $30+, so treat these as published starting rates, not take-home pay.

Practical tips:

  • The Starter Assessment can only be taken once, according to the FAQ, so read the instructions carefully before you begin [DataAnnotation FAQ].
  • Work availability depends on your skills and performance, so don't plan your budget around a fixed number of hours.
  • Legitimate platforms don't charge sign-up fees; be wary of any "AI training job" that asks you to pay.

Strategy 3: Fractional leadership roles

If you have senior experience, fractional work means serving as a part-time executive for one or more companies. Go Fractional's 2026 State of Fractional Work report puts the average posted hourly rate at $176 for fractional CMOs and $211 for fractional CTOs, with 25th-to-75th percentile ranges of $125 to $200 and $150 to $250 [Go Fractional, 2026]. Its live rate pages, which update continuously, currently show $175 and $209 [Go Fractional rates], so expect these numbers to drift.

Practical tips:

  • Define a clear scope (for example, a set number of hours per week and specific outcomes) before quoting.
  • Start with your existing network; fractional roles often come from people who already know your work.
  • Check your current employment agreement for conflict-of-interest or moonlighting rules.

Strategy 4: Local services and coaching, priced by where you sell

Where you sell can matter as much as what you sell. Thumbtack, a marketplace for local pros, reports a national average of $164 an hour for professional photography services [Thumbtack]. Upwork's global cost guide lists photographers at $25 to $45 an hour in median hourly rates [Upwork, Photographers]. These come from different data sets and job types, so they aren't a direct comparison, but they show how much the channel shapes pricing.

Coaching shows a similar spread. Upwork lists business coaches at $75 to $150 an hour in median hourly rates, with its guide describing entry-level coaches at $50 to $75 and experts at $200+ [Upwork, Business Coaches].

Practical tips:

  • For in-person services, list on local-focused platforms and your own Google Business Profile, not only global freelance sites.
  • For coaching, pick a narrow audience (new managers, first-time founders) and a defined package instead of open-ended sessions.
  • Don't present yourself as a licensed professional (therapist, financial adviser, attorney) unless you are one.

Strategy 5: Digital products you build once

Digital products such as printables, templates and short guides take time to make up front, but each extra copy costs little to deliver. That's the appeal: once a product is finished, it can keep selling without new production work for every order. The trade-off is that sales depend on people finding it, so marketing never fully stops.

A small example: the Calm Money Monthly Planner is a $5 printable I sell on Payhip. It was built once and is delivered as an instant download, so there's no shipping or inventory.

Practical tips:

  • Solve one specific problem for one specific group instead of making a generic product.
  • Use Pinterest or a simple blog to bring in steady traffic, since marketplaces alone may not.
  • Check each platform's fees and rules (including AI-disclosure rules where they apply) before you price.

Before you start: taxes and expectations

Side income is still income. In the U.S., the IRS sets the self-employment tax rate at 15.3%, made up of 12.4% for Social Security and 2.9% for Medicare, and you generally owe it once net self-employment earnings reach $400 or more [IRS]. The IRS also notes that you may need to make quarterly estimated payments. Rules depend on your situation, so check the IRS page or a qualified tax professional rather than relying on a blog post (including this one).

And remember Bankrate's median: $200 a month [Bankrate, 2025]. Plan for a slow start and treat higher numbers as a possible upside, not a baseline.

Conclusion

The data points in a consistent direction: demand is growing where people combine AI tools with real skills, experienced professionals can sell their time in fractional slices, local services and coaching depend heavily on the channel you sell through, and digital products reward patient, specific work. Pick one path that fits the skills and hours you actually have, start small, track what each hour earns, and adjust from there.
